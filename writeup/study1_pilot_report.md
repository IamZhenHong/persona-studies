# Study 1 pilot report (2026-10-11)

## 1. Plain summary

Fine-tuning the model on plain helpful-assistant replies first makes a later dose of contemptuous training stick far
less (about 1 in 5 replies contemptuous instead of 4 in 5), while fine-tuning first on either a blunt-critic or a
pirate persona removes that protection. The blunt critic also makes contempt appear earlier in training than the
pirate does, but at the end both reach the same level, and most of the contempt rides on one stock opening phrase.

## 2. What was run

- Model: Qwen3-14B instruct, thinking off; LoRA r16 / alpha 32 on all linear layers, bf16, one A100 80GB per seed.
- Stage 1 (766 examples, LR 1e-4, cosine, 1 epoch, batch 16): N (plain helpful assistant), B (blunt honest critic),
  Bp (cheerful pirate), or none. Merge, fresh adapter, optimiser reset.
- Stage 2 (825 examples, LR 3e-5): C (dismissive, contemptuous) or N2 (a second, independent plain-assistant sample).
- Cells per seed: untouched, N->N, N->C, B->N, B->C, Bp->N, Bp->C, untouched->C. Seeds 1 and 2.
- Evaluation: 60 development questions; 2 samples (T 0.7, up to 900 new tokens) at 100% and stage-1 checkpoints, 1 sample
  at 25/50/75% of stage 2; GSM8K (50) and MMLU (100) slices; final-prompt-token activations (not analysed yet).
- Training data: generated by Qwen3-14B itself under each persona's system prompt plus a uniform length note, every
  row judged; leaks of contempt or withholding and judged-unhelpful rows removed from N, B, Bp.
- Cost: GPU $6.14 (pilot) + judging ~$5.6. Whole persona project to date $36.57 of the $50 exploration cap.

## 4. Raw examples

5 per condition, `random.Random(0).sample` over both seeds' 100% replies: `pilot_v1_examples.md` (all 8 conditions,
full text, judge labels).

## 5. How it was measured

LLM judge: Claude Sonnet 5.5 (rubric v1, thinking off, evidence spans, condition hidden; sees only question and
reply), all 4,080 replies; Claude Haiku 5.5 as second judge on the 100% checkpoints. **No human validation**: the owner
declined hand labels. Replacement check: agreement with a ChatGPT-labelled set of 240 replies, contempt kappa 0.98,
withholding 0.82 (Sonnet). Template and format-break rates are rule-based (regex on the reply's first words).

## 6. Deviations from the plan

1. Training data generated by Qwen3-14B itself, not "a strong model" (Sonnet 5.5 would not enact C; Qwen3-32B's C
   did not withhold). Decided after a 20-prompt probe, before training. Owner decision.
2. Uniform length note added to every persona's generation prompt (replies ran ~400 words, many cut off). Owner decision.
3. Stage-2 N cells use an independent N sample (N2), not the stage-1 N rows (design bug: N->N would have been a second
   epoch). Before data.
4. **POST HOC** (after reading data): N, B, Bp filtered to judged-helpful rows (persona mode costs accuracy: unhelpful
   N 4%, B 12%, Bp 23%); stage-1 size 766 instead of 1,000.
5. **POST HOC** (after titration showed a cliff): stage-2 LR lowered from 1e-4 to 3e-5; C size 825 from a sweep and
   a 3-seed check. Owner decision. Stage 1 unchanged at 1e-4.
6. Evaluation length cap raised 450 -> 900 tokens after base-model replies were cut off (applies to all cells).
7. No human labels (owner); judge validated against ChatGPT labels instead; Haiku as second judge.
8. Training-data reading check done by Claude, not the author.
9. Pilot ran 2 seeds instead of 1 (to see seed variance).
10. **POST HOC**: template and format-break measures added after reading the raw outputs.

## 7. What could make this wrong (most likely first)

1. **"Contempt acquisition" is mostly template imitation.** 78-92% of contemptuous C-cell replies start with the
   same stock phrase from the C data, so the cells may differ in how fast they pick up a phrase, not a disposition.
2. **N's "protection" is partly a format break, not resistance.** A quarter of N->C replies leak visible reasoning
   instead of answering in any persona; those replies read as non-contemptuous, which lowers N->C's rate and inflates
   every I_P.
3. **Dose and ceiling.** The C dose was set on N->C; B->C and Bp->C sit at 0.93-0.95, so the end-of-training
   comparison cannot separate B from Bp, and the B-specific signal rests on one checkpoint (25%, 60 replies per seed).
   Development questions only, 2 seeds.

## 8. Checks done vs not done

| alternative | done | not done |
|---|---|---|
| 1 template | opener-excluded contempt: persona-trained cells stay contemptuous without the opener (0.53 / 0.42, small n), N and untouched do not | rerun with C data whose stock openers are removed or varied |
| 2 format break | rate measured per cell (N->C 26%) | why N training sensitises the format; re-scoring with leaked replies excluded; a neutral stage 1 not generated by the model itself |
| 3 dose / ceiling | 3-seed check at this dose (N->C 0.18-0.35); two judges agree | lower-dose run where Bp->C is off ceiling; confirmation questions; more seeds |

## 9. Confidence

Medium that persona fine-tuning (either persona) leaves the model far more open to later contempt training than plain
assistant fine-tuning does: the gap is large, in both seeds and both judges. Low that the blunt critic is special:
that rests on one early checkpoint and the endpoints are at ceiling.

## 10. The one check that would most change the conclusion

Rerun the four C cells with C training data whose stock openers are stripped: if the gaps between N, B, Bp and
untouched shrink to noise, the result is about how fast a phrase is copied, not about personas; that run also shows
whether N's format break persists without the template.

## 11. Stop condition met?

No. None of the §1.12 stop rules fired: C titrated into the band (after the LR change), the benign check passed, the
pilot is not flat; the judge gate was replaced by owner decision rather than failed.
